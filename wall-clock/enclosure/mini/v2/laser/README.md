## Cut the coupon first

The whole design rests on one number nobody can compute for you: how deep to
engrave so the wood glows without going through. It depends on your sheet, its
glue line, your laser and your lens.

`face-60-depth-test.svg` is **88 × 52.5 mm** and holds six columns:

| column | what it is |
|---|---|
| **0** | **not engraved at all.** The honest reference for "is this one glowing" |
| 1–5 | five settings, each in **its own colour** so the Glowforge gives each its own passes |

Each of the five is a **pair**: the 1.80 mm minute line and the 2.80 mm hour
line, side by side, at the **real 30.5 mm length**. Both, because a wider slot
glows brighter at the same depth — a setting that suits one can be wrong for
the other, and testing only the narrow one is how you find that out after
cutting a 232 mm disc. Full length, because the question is not only "does it
glow" but "does it glow *evenly along the line*".

The numerals are engraved too, in a **sixth colour, all at one shallow
setting** — a label that got deeper along with the patch it labels would be
illegible at exactly the setting you most want to identify.

Set columns 1→5 to increasing passes (or decreasing speed). Cut it, take it
into a dark room, hold it over a lit LED.

You are looking for the column that **glows evenly and still looks like wood**.
One step past that it goes translucent and grey at the edges; two steps past,
it breaks through. Write the winner down — it is the setting for the face, and
it is the only part of this job that cannot be got right by measuring the
model.

**Be honest with yourself about the arithmetic before you start.** 3 mm of ply
less the ~0.5 mm you want to leave is **2.5 mm of wood to take away by raster**,
and the Aura is a diode laser. That is a long, hot job and it may char the line
edges brown before it gets deep enough. If the coupon says so, say the word and
I will re-cut the geometry for a 1.5 mm sheet — `PLY_T` drives both the face and
the ring behind it, so it is a regeneration, not a redraw.

## Then the face

Engrave, then cut, **in that order** — the file is written so the engraving
comes first, because a disc that has already been cut free will lift or shift.

**Engrave the lines from the BACK.** The front stays clean wood; the glow comes
through the veneer that is left.

You cannot get the orientation wrong, and that is deliberate rather than lucky:
every feature in both face files is symmetric about the 12–6 axis, so the
drawing and its mirror image are identical. `check11` tests exactly that. The
plain face is 60-fold symmetric on top of it — any of the sixty rotations seats
it correctly.

## Material

**Never PVC, never vinyl, and never "fire-retardant treated" ply.** Chlorinated
material in a laser releases hydrogen chloride: it ruins the optics, corrodes
the machine and is a genuine hazard. This has been the standing rule on this
build since the first BOM. Plain birch ply or basswood.

3 mm nominal. Real "3 mm" plywood runs 2.7–3.3 — **measure your sheet**. The
seat is 3.01 mm below the clock's front face, so the face sits proud or shy by
whatever your sheet differs from that.

## How it fits — all measured off the built mesh, not the drawing

| | |
|---|---|
| clock front face | z = 22.00 |
| front bore | r = 116.50 (Ø233.00) |
| **the seat** | the screen collar, r 30.65–35.10, **top at z = 18.99** |
| the disc | Ø232.40 — 0.30 mm radial clearance, ~0.20 once the kerf has its half |
| screen window | Ø54.00, 1 mm inside the 55 mm active area, so the face covers the panel edge |
| the lines | 60 × 1.80 mm wide, r 80.0 → 110.5, every 6°, one at 12 o'clock |

The seat was already there. `Z_RECESS` in `params.py` has read *"plywood face
recess floor"* since the first version of this project; nothing had ever been
cut for it.

## One printed part changes

**Print `mini-round-clock-diffuser-60-cells` instead of
`mini-round-clock-diffuser-60-plain`.**

The wood is opaque, so it does not need the white diffuser's cell walls to keep
light off the face — it *is* the mask. But the cells do a second job, and that
one still matters: **they stop LED n lighting tick n+1.** Take them away and one
lit LED glows through three or four lines, and the hands stop being hands.

`-cells` is the same part with the front 3 mm sliced off: no membrane, no face,
just the sixty cells, the pockets that hold the perspex light guides, and the
band that presses into the ring pocket. It tops out at z 18.93 and the wood sits
at 18.99, so they meet. 30.6 cm³, prints sliced-face-down, no support.

## Assembly

1. Ring, display and wiring as usual.
2. `diffuser-60-cells` into the ring pocket.
3. The sixty perspex light guides into its cells. They rest on the shelf at
   z 15.50 and the cell walls hold them square.
4. The plywood face in, engraved side down. It lands on the screen collar and
   its front comes flush with the clock's.
5. Retain it — the bore locates it but does not hold it. A dab of glue at three
   or four points on the collar is enough, and keeps it removable.

## What has not been verified

Nothing here has been cut. The fit, the line positions and the seat are measured
against the built meshes by `check11_face.py`; the engrave depth, the char and
whether 3 mm is simply too much to raster away are questions only the coupon can
answer, on your machine, with your wood.

# The cut-through build, with printed plugs

Sam, 2026-09-17: *"I will be cutting out the 60 lines instead of engraving.
Create a 3d printed file that can be pushed into the 60 cut lines from the back.
I want them to be individual pieces that are a press fit. They will be printed
in white."*

This replaces the engrave-depth problem with a press-fit problem, and it is the
better trade. `PLY_GLOW_LEFT` could only ever be found by burning coupons and
then living with whatever the sheet did on the day; a through-cut is a
through-cut. **If you are doing this, `face-60-depth-test.svg` is no longer
relevant** — the engrave depth is not a question any more.

## Do the fit test first, and do it on the real sheet

The slot the laser leaves is the line it was given **plus its kerf** — about
0.20 in 3 mm ply, so a 1.80 line leaves roughly a 2.00 hole. About. Kerf depends
on the sheet, the lens, the power and the speed, and a plug sized from a guess
at it will either rattle or split the ply.

So: cut `face-60-slot-test.svg` **from the same sheet, on the same settings you
will cut the face with**, print the six test plugs, and press them in.

- **1** is 1.95, **6** is 2.20, in 0.05 steps.
- You want the one that needs a firm push and then stays put. Not the one you
  can push with a thumb, and not the one that needs a mallet — the ply splits
  along the grain at the end of a slot and you will not see it until the face
  is cut.
- Tell me the number and I will rebuild the sixty at that width. It is one
  parameter (`PLUG_FIT`); nothing else moves.

The test plugs have a **thick flange with the number raised on it** so you can
tell them apart and get a grip. That flange is deliberately too thick for the
clock — the real ones are 0.40.

## The plug

Stadium body, the slot's own section, with a wider thinner flange behind it.
Pushed in **from the back**: the body fills the 3 mm of ply and finishes flush
at the front, the flange stops against the wood's back face so it cannot go
through, and gives you something to push on that is not the 2 mm sliver itself.

| | |
|---|---|
| body | **2.10** × 31.60, 3.00 tall (= `PLY_T`, the sheet) |
| flange | **5.60** × 32.00, **0.40** thick |
| interference | 0.10 on width only |

**Why the flange is 0.40 and no more.** Measured off the seated cell ring, not
chosen: the pocket behind each tick is 6.65 wide and 3.25 deep, the wood's back
face is at z 19.00, and a fitted 3 mm perspex guide tops out at 18.50 — leaving
**0.43**. So 0.40 clears the guides if they are in and obviously clears if they
are not. One part, either build.

**Why it is only wider at the sides, not the ends.** The slot already reaches
r 79.10 and the guide channel wall starts at 79.00. There is room at the sides
and none at the ends.

## Printing

White, and the whiter and more translucent the better — the plug *is* the
diffuser now.

Lay them **flange-down on the bed**. The flange is a flat 5.60 × 32.00 footprint
and the body rises 3 mm from the middle of it: no overhangs, no supports, and
the layer lines run across the light path rather than along it. 60 of them is
about 16 cm³.

The plate is sixty **separate bodies** with no sprue, because Sam asked for
individual pieces and a sprue on a 2 mm part breaks the part when you cut it.
Your slicer will treat the file as one object with sixty parts.

## Assembly order

1. Cut the face. **The outline is the last cut in the file** and that matters
   more here than it did for the engraved version — a disc with sixty
   through-slots in it is a lot more fragile, and a piece that comes free early
   takes the gantry with it.
2. Lay the face **face-down** on something soft.
3. Press the sixty plugs in from the back. They finish flush at the front.
4. Then seat the whole face onto the clock, on the collar as before.

`diffuser-60-cells` is still the part behind it — the wood is the mask, but the
cells are what stop LED n lighting tick n+1.
