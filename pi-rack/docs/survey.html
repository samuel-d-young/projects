What Pis Are Actually For

Ten categories researched against the live web. 183 distinct uses catalogued, each annotated against a real existing setup. Then two synthesis passes with opposite instincts — one cataloguing, one sceptical.

Almost everything the internet tells you to put on a Raspberry Pi, you already run better somewhere else. The Pi jobs that survive are the ones that need a separate box — or a box in a different building.

The useful part

Four gaps nothing had caught

Absent from the stack and from the plan written for it last week — which makes these the survey's real output.

There is no remote access at all

No VPN, no tunnel, no overlay network anywhere on the network. Everything worth reaching is LAN-only, for someone who is away from the house for entire days.

Tailscale, on the NUC and the workstation — minutes. Its April 2026 pricing change removed the 100-device cap from the free personal plan entirely.

No password manager, while holding clients' logins

Meta Business Suite and Google Business credentials for multiple dental practices. That is a carried liability.

Vaultwarden — 66.4k stars, Rust, ~100 MB. A legitimate Pi job precisely because it should not sit alongside everything else.

No hardware security keys

Best value-per-minute in the entire survey, and not a Pi at all.

Two FIDO2 keys — one primary, one in a drawer. Twenty minutes, then never maintained again.

No out-of-band console

A headless hypervisor runs the Home Assistant VM that 2005 entities depend on. If it fails to POST, recovery means carrying a monitor to it.

PiKVM (V3 HAT, Pi 4 only) — or just buy a JetKVM at about US$103 and skip the build.

NOAA APT weather satellites are gone. NOAA-18 was decommissioned 6 June 2025, NOAA-19 on 13 August, NOAA-15 on 19 August. The last APT transmitter in orbit is silent — so roughly half of every “SDR projects for Raspberry Pi” list is now instructions for receiving nothing. Meteor M2-series LRPT still transmits; APT does not.

RPi.GPIO is dead on the Pi 5. The GPIO registers moved behind the RP1 southbridge, so poking /dev/mem cannot reach them. Any tutorial beginning import RPi.GPIO simply fails. Use gpiozero or lgpio.

And watch for repos that moved, where search still surfaces the corpse: Homepage (benphelps → gethomepage), Homebox (hay-kot → sysadminsmedia), BirdNET.

Verified, not assumed

Google archived the entire Coral toolchain

Confirmed by fetching the repositories directly: pycoral archived 3 July 2025, libedgetpu archived 14 October 2025. Frigate no longer recommends Coral for new installs and points builders at Hailo instead.

This is not a reason to replace a working Coral — and the survey caught me being too enthusiastic about that earlier in the day. A Coral doing 11.5 ms across six cameras with skipped_fps: 0.0 is fine; the Hailo-8L benches around 10–13 ms. That is a lateral move at higher cost, and several users report Coral is better at night under IR illumination.

Archived is not broken. The archive matters for the next build. If a Coral dies or the camera count grows, the AI HAT+ (Hailo-8L ~$70, Hailo-8 ~$110) is the succession path.

Popular, and wrong here

Traps

  • Jellyfin or Plex on a Pi. Jellyfin's own docs now carry a deprecation notice for Pi V4L2 acceleration, citing the Pi 5's total lack of an H.264 encoder. Plex is worse — a subscription for something the hardware cannot do.
  • A Pi as router or firewall. pfSense and OPNsense are x86-only, so it is not a config question, it is impossible as stated. And a Pi 4 has one NIC.
  • Nextcloud. PHP stats every file individually against a network mount — the known-worst deployment shape — to duplicate things a NAS already does.
  • A k3s cluster on four Pi 4s. When every workload is pinned to specific hardware — an accelerator, a set of mounts, a VM with USB passthrough — there is nothing left to schedule.
  • Ollama on a Pi CPU. 2–5 tok/s, and published benchmarks are first-run numbers: without active cooling a Pi 5 halves throughput within about 90 seconds.
  • LanCache on a Pi. Measured at 30–40 MB/s with USB storage — slower than a decent NBN connection, so the cache makes downloads worse.
  • MagicMirror². The framework is alive; the project is the problem — two-way glass, a frame, a monitor and a wall, for a clock you already have three of.

Framing

The power argument is weaker than folklore

A Pi 5 idles around 3 W; an N100 mini PC around 6–8 W. Run continuously, that difference is roughly $10 a year — not the order of magnitude people imply.

So if you already own spare boards and spare mini PCs, “cheaper to run” is not a real reason to choose a Pi. The honest reasons are GPIO, HATs, camera modules, and physical separation — a box that can fail without taking anything else with it, or a box that lives somewhere else entirely.
