Before the options

Four things the survey found on the machine

None of these were designed. They were measured while grounding the ideas, and they reorder the list on their own.

Fault

resolve-sync is protecting nothing

auto_sync: true and synced_projects: [] are independent settings, so nothing errors and nothing warns. Three projects were pushed by hand; the config was last written 29 July. Nine of your twelve most recently modified projects are in no store at all, including NZ 360 Footage and R2W & R2B 20.8.

Fault

The next wedding does not fit

Twelve real jobs measured: median 278 GB. K: has 46.7 GB free, L: 151.8, V: 20.1 — 219 GB between them, 0.79 of one job. O: has 10.7 TB, so the machine is not out of disk. It is out of disk where the work is filed, which is why it stays invisible until a card will not offload at a venue at midnight.

Fault

Ten projects point at a drive that is not mounted

All from Feb–Jun 2024. Six have a plausible media folder elsewhere; four have nothing findable on any mounted drive, and one of those is a wedding (bREE & eTHAN). Twenty minutes of checking, not a catastrophe — and getting that distinction right is the whole design brief.

Gift

MediaPool:AutoSyncAudio() exists

Waveform-correlation multicam sync — what PluralEyes sells for around $400 — is one documented call in the Studio scripting API you already own. That turns option 6 from a signal-processing project into a scripting job.

The list

Ordered by consequence × odds you finish it

Not by how interesting they are to build. Each has a first evening you can actually do, and a kill criterion — an option with no kill criterion is a trap.

  1. 01

    Deadman

    Tonight 0 Pi ~3 hours

    Make resolve-sync structurally unable to report that it is protecting your projects while watching none of them.

    Tick the projects (two minutes). Then make the agent derive its protected status rather than assert it, and say so out loud if it ever goes empty again. That third part is a product fix for every editor you ship this to — anyone can hit it, and the failure is silent by construction.

    Kill criterion: none for the first two parts. It is a config fix and a bug fix.

  2. 02

    Ledger

    Does not exist Prototype runs 1 Pi ~20 hours

    What is safe to delete, what exists exactly once, and which project had the Boronia ceremony wide.

    One index over Resolve's own project database, the job folders, and which volumes are mounted — reporting where they disagree. Run tonight it found the ten dead-path projects, 25 of 34 job folders with no matching project, and 260 loose media files belonging to no job (145 in K:\2026 alone).

    The Pi holds the database, the UI and the schedule. The volume walk and the hashing run on the workbench — a Pi in the data path is 120+ hours per pass.

    First evening: not code. Find out whether F: is unplugged, re-lettered or gone. Kill if the first census shows most of Weddings already has a second copy.

  3. 03

    Freeboard

    Prototype runs 0 Pi ~8 hours

    Not a disk-space alert. “Will Saturday's job fit in the place it actually goes?”

    Every tool can tell you a volume is 99% full, and you have learned to ignore all of them. This needs three things a generic alert lacks: what a job costs (measured), which volumes count, and a verdict rather than a percentage. Its answer on your machine right now:

    VERDICT WILL NOT FIT

    First evening: no code — empty $RECYCLE.BIN (76.5 GB) and CacheClip (72.7 GB), open a second job root on M:. Kill if that gets you past ~560 GB free.

  4. 04

    Tithe

    0 Pi ~12 hours (tier 0 is one evening)

    Stop trying to back up 49 TiB. You do not have 49 TiB of irreplaceable data.

    Delivered masters, every .drp, the repos, the brain vault, property-watch's unbackfillable database, the receipts. That set is small. The text-shaped part is a few gigabytes and can be in a Backblaze bucket with Object Lock before bed, for about the price of a coffee a month — and provider-enforced immutability beats any append-only flag on a box in the same house.

    Kill if the classifier's irreplaceable bin comes out above ~4 TB. The rule set is wrong, not the budget.

  5. 05

    Housebox

    1 Pi ~2 hours the embarrassingly simple one

    Your own DMX controller, permanently on a Pi. Zero new code — you already wrote the installer.

    N:\DMX Controller ships pi_install.sh: venv, FastAPI, UDP 6454, TCP 8080, systemd unit with Restart=on-failure. And main.py exposes plain GET endpoints clearly built for a Stream Deck:

    /sd/blackout   /sd/full   /sd/preset/{id}   /sd/scene/{name}

    Scene recall is a URL — so Home Assistant drives the rig with a one-line rest_command, and a physical GO button is one HTTP call.

    Two cautions: static IP outside any venue DHCP pool, and keep it separate from xLights — xlights_rgbeffects.xml has zero models in it, so a show build is a much larger and currently speculative project.

  6. 06

    Chorus

    Does not exist 0 Pi ~14–30 hours

    Come home from the wedding to a Resolve project already synced, marked and waiting.

    Worth doing now because the hard part turns out to be written already: AutoSyncAudio in AUDIO_SYNC_WAVEFORM mode is a documented single call. What remains is import, grouping, timeline assembly and markers. Realistically 1–2 hours per wedding across ~25 weddings — the largest recurring saving in the pool.

    Kill if waveform and timecode results disagree by more than ~2 frames on over 10% of clips across two weddings.

  7. 07

    On the Record

    Does not exist 0 Pi ~10 hours has an invoice attached

    A dated, hash-chained register of what Dental Story published, for which practice, on which date.

    AHPRA complaints arrive a year later, and Instagram lets history be quietly edited. Section 133 now carries a maximum of AUD $60,000 per offence for an individual (up from $5,000), in force in every jurisdiction including WA since July 2024 — and AHPRA's enforcement strategy treats “the person or entity who controls part or all of the advertising” as the advertiser. You write the copy. Australind is in WA.

    Every existing AHPRA tool audits a live website after publication. None keeps a per-post record of why something passed. The hook already exists: eight Posted/ folders, a convention you invented.

    Kill criterion, and it is real: show one page to a lawyer or your insurer before it goes near a client. A self-maintained register could create an expectation you do not want. Not legal advice.

  8. 08

    Roar

    Does not exist 0 Pi ~20 hours compounds over time

    The room writes your edit markers. Record it, analyse it afterwards, hand every camera card a marker file saying where the good bits were.

    AddMarker(...customData) means the tool can tag its own markers and later delete only its own, without touching yours. It gets better the longer it runs — after twenty events you know things about your own rooms nobody else does.

    Kill criterion: measure camera clock drift on one past job first. One hour, and it decides the project.

  9. 09

    Customs

    Does not exist 1 Pi 4 h to decide · 40 h to build

    Does the card actually contain valid files — not just faithfully copied bytes?

    Every offload tool answers “did the bytes copy correctly”. Hedge and ShotPut do it with checksums; Little Backup Box not even that. None opens the file to ask whether the camera wrote a valid one. That is the cleanest verified gap in the whole pool.

    But do not build the box first. Build the four-hour script that walks ten years of footage and counts how many files are actually malformed. That number decides the other thirty-six hours.

    Kill if the script finds zero malformed files across ten years. The box would guard a failure that has never happened to you.

  10. 10

    Kit Wall

    Does not exist 0 Pi (ESP32s) ~16 hours

    At 5am the garage says the drone case and one mic bag are still on the shelf. At 11pm it says the second body never came home.

    The gear-tracking market is checkout ledgers — a human scans a barcode on the way out. This is sensors: reed switches in printed cradles, so the shelf knows what is on it without anyone remembering to tell it. You have two printers and an ESPHome fleet.

    First evening: two slots, not twelve. Print one cradle and test magnet alignment before printing sixteen.

The boards

What the five Pis end up doing

Only three options claim a board, and one is co-tenancy. Stated plainly rather than dressed up.

BoardJob
Pi 4 — gaffercanary + watchdog (unchanged)
Pi 4 — sparksESPHome build server (unchanged — and Kit Wall needs it)
Pi 4 — runnertravelling LAN kit (unchanged) + Housebox
Pi 4 — the boxed oneLedger — the always-on index and web UI
Pi 5 — standbyFrigate warm spare, or Customs if the four-hour script justifies it

The Pi 5 is the only board whose two USB3 ports do not share one controller — exactly what a card-in-one-port, SSD-in-the-other copy needs. So if Customs survives its test, that is where it goes.

Cut

Six ideas that died for good reasons

47 were cut in total. These are the ones you would most likely have wanted to build.

A wireless-mic dropout detectorYour Hollyland Lark transmitters are already 32-bit float onboard recorders marketed explicitly as dropout backups, and the system auto-selects the least congested channel. The gear you own solves it.
A gear-bag NFC trackerCRDBAG Gear Organizer and ShootPrep already exist, aimed at exactly your profession.
An LED timecode beaconRocSync (arXiv 2511.14948) does it with a better encoding than the one proposed.
A comment-moderation bot for dental clientsInstagram's own Hidden Words filter and AHPRA's free testimonial tool give you roughly 80% of it this afternoon, for no code.
A stage timer for LAN eventscpvalente/ontime is free, open source and self-hostable. Put it on a Pi and spend the afternoon on Roar.
A duplicate finder for the Dental Story folderInstall Czkawka and delete them. Writing a deduplicator is a classic satisfying trap — and automated deletion on a 99.6%-full volume with no offsite copy is genuinely dangerous.
